# SLIP-0027 : Transaction verification statements

```
Number:  SLIP-0027
Title:   Transaction verification statements
Type:    Standard
Status:  Draft
Authors: Petr Susil <petr.susil@satoshilabs.com>
Created: 2026-09-29
```

## Abstract

A transaction statement is a human-readable account of the security-relevant effects of the outputs of a transaction, such as the assets the payer sends or receives, which a wallet displays before it authorizes the transaction.
An attestation is a commitment to a statement, established by evidence which the wallet verifies under a trust anchor it holds.
The attestation is issued by a party or mechanism which the wallet trusts for that purpose, and which need not be the party that requested the payment.

## Motivation

A wallet can authenticate the destination and the amount of an output, but that does not establish what the output does.
Bitcoin `OP_RETURN` data and EVM calldata are transaction data which an address and an amount do not describe, and whose meaning depends on a protocol the wallet may not implement.
A wallet which cannot establish the effects of such an output can only present it as opaque data (*blind signing*) or refuse to sign it.

This specification defines a statement, which describes those effects in a form the wallet can display, and an attestation, which allows the verification of the statement to be performed elsewhere and checked by the wallet.
A statement in a payment request is trusted as far as the payment request signer is; an attestation establishes it independently.

A statement and its attestation are defined independently of any payment request, so that they can also be used when a transaction is signed without one.

## Statement

A statement is the concatenation of the following fields:

* *summary* (length-prefixed UTF-8 string): a short description of the action, such as "Swap on Uniswap", written by the party which makes the statement.
* *n* (CompactSize integer): the number of effects which follow. The CompactSize integer MUST be encoded in the fewest possible number of bytes.
* *effect*<sub>1</sub> || *effect*<sub>2</sub> || ... || *effect*<sub>*n*</sub> (variable length): concatenation of the binary encodings of the effects, in ascending lexicographic order of their encodings. An effect MUST NOT be repeated.

An effect is encoded as the concatenation of the following fields:

* *effectType* (1 byte): the kind of effect, see below.
* *coinType* (4 bytes): 32-bit encoding of the SLIP-0044 coin type of the asset in little-endian byte order.
* *asset* (length-prefixed binary string): the identifier of the asset under *coinType*. A zero-length string denotes the native asset of *coinType*. For EVM coin types, a token is identified by the 20-byte address of its contract, followed, for a non-fungible or multi-token contract, by the 32-byte token identifier in big-endian byte order.
* *bound* (1 byte): how *amount* bounds the effect: 0x00 exact, 0x01 at least, 0x02 at most, 0x03 unbounded.
* *amount* (*coinType*-dependent length): the amount in the smallest unit of the asset, encoded in the same way as the amount of a requested output in [SLIP-0024](slip-0024.md). It MUST be zero if *bound* is unbounded.
* *party* (length-prefixed string): the address of the counterparty of the effect, encoded in the same way as the address of a requested output in [SLIP-0024](slip-0024.md).

The following types of effects are defined:

| *effectType* | Effect | *party* | Permitted *bound* |
| ------------ | ------ | ------- | ----------------- |
| 0x01 | *send*: the asset is transferred from the payer to *party*. | recipient | exact, at most, unbounded |
| 0x02 | *receive*: the asset is delivered to *party*, which the payer controls. | payer's address | exact, at least |
| 0x03 | *approve*: *party* may transfer the asset from the payer, up to *amount*. | spender | exact, unbounded |
| 0x04 | *delegate*: *party* obtains authority over the asset, or over every asset of the payer under *coinType* if *asset* is a zero-length string. | delegate | unbounded |

The bound of a send, an approve or a delegate is an upper bound on the payer's loss, and the bound of a receive is a lower bound on the payer's gain.
An approve or a delegate is stated as if it does not lapse, since the current time is not reliably available in hardware wallets.
A security-relevant effect which none of these types describes cannot be expressed in a statement, and a method MUST NOT apply to a payload which has one.

A statement with no effects states that the payload has no security-relevant effect. The statement of a state assertion has no effects; the observed state is identified by the verification context.

All variable-length fields are encoded the same way as in Bitcoin transactions, as a [length-prefixed string](https://en.bitcoin.it/wiki/Protocol_documentation#Variable_length_string), where the length is encoded as a variable-length CompactSize integer.

## Displaying a statement

A wallet which relies on a statement, whether attested or part of a payment request, MUST display the summary and one line for each effect, in the order of the encoding.
The wording of each line is determined by the effect alone.
The summary MUST be displayed as a description provided by the party which makes the statement, separately from the effects, and the wallet MUST NOT present it as verified.

The canonical English rendering of an effect is:

| Effect | Rendering |
| ------ | --------- |
| send, exact | Send *amount* to *party* |
| send, at most | Send at most *amount* to *party* |
| send, unbounded | Send any amount of *asset* to *party* |
| receive, exact | Receive *amount* |
| receive, at least | Receive at least *amount* |
| approve, exact | Allow *party* to spend up to *amount* |
| approve, unbounded | Allow *party* to spend any amount of *asset* |
| delegate | Give *party* control of *asset*, or of the account if *asset* is a zero-length string |

A wallet MAY display a localized equivalent of the rendering.

The wallet formats *amount* and *asset* using asset metadata which it holds for the pair of *coinType* and *asset*. If the wallet holds no metadata for the asset, it MUST either display the asset identifier and the amount in the smallest unit, or refuse the statement.
An unbounded effect MUST be displayed in a way which distinguishes it from a bounded one.

The wallet MUST verify that it controls the *party* of a receive, for example by means of an address authentication code as described in [SLIP-0024](slip-0024.md), and MUST reject the statement otherwise. It MAY display the account instead of the address.

The wallet MUST reject a statement which is malformed or non-canonical, has trailing data, or violates a constraint of [Statement](#statement).
The wallet MAY check an effect against facts which it establishes itself, such as the amount it transfers in the transaction being signed, and MUST reject a statement which contradicts them.

## Attestation

An attestation is the concatenation of the following fields:

* *payloadHash* (32 bytes): commitment to the outputs to which the statement applies, see [Payload](#payload).
* *statement* (variable length): the attested statement, encoded as defined in [Statement](#statement).
* *verificationMethod* (4 bytes): 32-bit identifier of the verification method in little-endian byte order, see [Verification methods](#verification-methods).
* *attester* (length-prefixed binary string): identifier of the trust anchor under which the evidence for *attestationHash* is verified, such as the public key of a signing service, the measurement of a trusted execution environment, or the identity of a server whose response is proven, see [Evidence](#evidence).
* *verificationData* (length-prefixed binary string): method-specific parameters and evidence.

## Payload

The binary encoding of an output is defined in [SLIP-0024](slip-0024.md), *Signature generation*, and is used here whether or not a payment request is present.

Where the attestation is used with a payment request, the *payload* is the requested outputs of that payment request; otherwise it is every output of the transaction being signed. In either case the outputs are taken in the order in which they appear.

```
payloadHash = SHA256(output_1 || output_2 || ... || output_n)
```

where *output*<sub>*i*</sub> is the binary encoding of the *i*-th output of the payload. With a payment request, *payloadHash* is therefore equal to *outputsHash* of [SLIP-0024](slip-0024.md).

An attestation whose *payloadHash* is SHA-256 of the empty string is a *state assertion*; it applies to no output.
The wallet MUST compute *payloadHash* itself from the transaction being signed, and MUST reject an attestation whose *payloadHash* is neither the computed value nor that of a state assertion.

## Auxiliary data

*Auxiliary data* is the facts outside the payload which the wallet supplies to the verification, namely:

* the *nonce*, an unpredictable challenge issued by the wallet;
* the *coinType*, the SLIP-0044 coin type under which the outputs are interpreted, which identifies the chain as defined in [SLIP-0024](slip-0024.md), *Coin type of EVM chains*;
  the wallet MUST refuse an attestation for a chain which has no registered coin type of its own;
* where the attestation is used with a payment request, memos of that payment request which affect the statement, such as a coin purchase memo.

The wallet MUST take auxiliary data from its own processing of the transaction and of the payment request, and MUST NOT take it from *verificationData*.
The wallet MUST NOT rely on an attestation whose nonce it did not issue or has invalidated, and MUST invalidate the nonce on acceptance.

## Verification context

The *verification context* is the canonical encoding of the auxiliary data and of every other fact outside the payload which can affect the statement.
It MUST determine every such fact and commit to any evidence from which one is established, and transaction facts MUST be checked against the transaction being signed.
It begins with b"\x53\x4c\x27\x43" ("SL'C", for context), followed by the 32-bit encoding of *coinType* in little-endian byte order and by the nonce; any method-specific context follows.

## Attestation hash

```
verificationContextHash = SHA256(verificationContext)
attestationHash = SHA256(
    b"\x53\x4c\x27\x41" ||
    verificationMethod ||
    attester ||
    payloadHash ||
    verificationContextHash ||
    statement
)
```

where b"\x53\x4c\x27\x41" is "SL'A", for attestation, *verificationMethod* is encoded as a 32-bit little-endian integer, *attester* is length-prefixed and *statement* is encoded as defined in [Statement](#statement).
*attestationHash* is not part of the attestation. The wallet MUST compute it from the payload and the verification context which it derives itself.

## Evidence

*attestationHash* MUST be established by evidence in *verificationData* which the wallet verifies under a trust anchor it holds for the method, such as an attester's signature,
a succinct proof or a hardware-attested execution. The wallet MUST NOT rely on an attestation unless the evidence establishes the value of *attestationHash* which it computed.

## Verification methods

A verification method determines both how the statement is derived, such as by interpretation by means of a descriptor or by simulation, and how the attestation is established, such as by a signature, a trusted execution environment or a proof of a server response; the same derivation established in different ways constitutes different methods.

A method MUST produce a *complete* statement, whose effects account for every *security-relevant effect* of the payload — any loss of the payer's assets, or any claim on them or on authority over them, now or later, such as a standing allowance.

A statement depends on the payload alone (*interpretation*), on the payload executed against an observed state (*simulation*), or on an observation of mutable state (*dynamic state*);
where a statement mixes these, the strongest dependence applies. For a statement to be complete, an interpretation MUST bound each effect under the semantics the method covers
or mark it unbounded; a simulation MUST bound each effect by a condition which transaction validity or execution enforces or mark it unbounded; a dynamic state MUST identify the observed state.

A method MUST define:

* the payloads or state assertions to which it applies;
* the evidence in *verificationData* and the trust anchor which *attester* identifies;
* the auxiliary data it uses and its encoding in the verification context;
* how it establishes the effects of the statement.

## Use with a payment request

A statement memo of a [SLIP-0024](slip-0024.md) payment request is a claim of the payment request signer. A wallet MAY require an attestation for it.
The nonce and *coinType* of the attestation are those of the payment request, its *statement* MUST be identical to the statement of the memo,
and the wallet MUST reject the payment request otherwise.
