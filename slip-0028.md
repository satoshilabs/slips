Title:   Transaction verification statements
Type:    Standard
Status:  Draft

         Suyash Shandilya <suyash.shandilya@satoshilabs.com>

## Abstract

A transaction statement is a human-readable account of the security-relevant effects of the outputs of a transaction, such as the assets the payer sends or which are delivered to an address, which a wallet displays before it authorizes the transaction, together with attributes of the parties and assets involved, such as the name of an address.
An attestation is a commitment to a statement, established by evidence which the wallet verifies under a trust anchor it holds.
The attestation is issued by a party or mechanism which the wallet trusts for that purpose, and which need not be the party that requested the payment.

## Motivation

A wallet can authenticate the destination and the amount of an output, but that does not establish what the output does.
Bitcoin `OP_RETURN` data and EVM calldata are transaction data which an address and an amount do not describe, and whose meaning depends on a protocol the wallet may not implement.
A wallet which cannot establish the effects of such an output can only present it as opaque data (*blind signing*) or refuse to sign it.

This specification defines a statement, which describes those effects in a form the wallet can display, and an attestation, which allows the verification of the statement to be performed elsewhere and checked by the wallet.
A statement in a payment request is trusted as far as the payment request signer is; an attestation establishes it independently.

A statement and its attestation are defined independently of any payment request, so that they can also be used when a transaction is signed without one.

## Chains

The address of an EVM account is the same on every EVM chain, and the same address can hold different code and different assets on each of them.
An address or a token contract therefore does not identify a chain, and a statement or an attestation which names one MUST identify its chain separately.
A SLIP-0044 coin type cannot do so reliably: SLIP-0044 does not map coin types to [EIP-155](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-155) chain IDs, a registered coin type such as 9000 (Avalanche) or 714 (BNB) need not denote the EVM chain of its ecosystem, EVM chains commonly share coin type 60 for key derivation, most EVM chains have no registered coin type, and coin type 1 is shared by the testnets of all coins.
[SLIP-0024](slip-0024.md) identifies the chain of a payment request by its coin type and refuses chains without a registered one, see [SLIP-0024](slip-0024.md), *Coin type of EVM chains*.

This specification instead identifies a chain by the chain type and the chain reference of [CAIP-350](https://github.com/ChainAgnostic/CAIPs/blob/main/CAIPs/caip-350.md), which form the chain part of an [ERC-7930](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-7930) Interoperable Address:

```
chain = chainType || chainRefLength || chainRef
```

* *chainType* (2 bytes): the CAIP-350 chain type of the namespace of the chain, in big-endian byte order, as listed below.
* *chainRefLength* (1 byte): the length of *chainRef*, which MUST NOT be zero.
* *chainRef*: the binary chain reference, as listed below.

| Namespace | *chainType* | *chainRef* |
| --------- | ----------- | ---------- |
| eip155 | 0x0000 | the non-zero EIP-155 chain ID as an unsigned integer in big-endian byte order without leading zero bytes |
| bip122 | 0x0001 | the first 16 bytes of the hash of the genesis block, in the byte order of its hexadecimal display form |

A chain of the eip155 namespace is an *EVM chain*.
These definitions follow the CAIP-350 profiles of the namespaces; this specification copies them and does not change with later revisions of the profiles.
The ERC-7930 Chain Identifier of a chain is 0x0001 || *chain* || 0x00.
The wallet MUST reject a chain whose *chainType* is not listed, whose *chainRef* is malformed or non-canonical, or which it does not support.

For example, Ethereum is 0x0000 || 0x01 || 0x01, Arbitrum One (chain ID 42161) is 0x0000 || 0x02 || 0xa4b1, Bitcoin is 0x0001 || 0x10 || 0x000000000019d6689c085ae165831e93, and Bitcoin testnet3 is 0x0001 || 0x10 || 0x000000000933ea01ad0ee984209779ba.

### Encodings

| Namespace | *account* | *asset* | *txType* | Payload coin type |
| --------- | --------- | ------- | -------- | ----------------- |
| eip155 | 20-byte address | empty for the native asset, or the 20-byte contract address, followed by the 32-byte big-endian token identifier for a non-fungible or multi-token contract | [EIP-2718](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-2718) type, 0x00 if legacy | an EVM coin type |
| bip122 | `scriptPubKey` | empty | 0x00 | the SLIP-0044 coin type of the chain |

Accounts on the same chain are equal if and only if their encodings are; on EVM chains, this includes the contract address in the first 20 bytes of an *asset*.
The payload is encoded as requested outputs of [SLIP-0024](slip-0024.md) for the payload coin type. A payload attestation applies only to a transaction on a chain listed above.

## Statement

A statement consists of a summary and of entries, each of which is an effect or an attribute:

* An *effect* is a change which the successful execution of the payload makes, or causes a protocol to make afterwards, such as a transfer of an asset or an authority granted over it.
* An *attribute* is a fact about a party or an asset which holds when the statement is made, such as the name of an address.

### Format

```
statement = summary || n || entry_1 || ... || entry_n
entry     = entryType || chain || body
```

* *summary* (length-prefixed UTF-8 string): a short description of the action by the party which makes the statement.
* *n* (CompactSize integer): the number of entries, which MUST be distinct and in ascending lexicographic order of their encodings.
* *entryType* (1 byte): the type of the entry, below 0x80 for an effect and from 0x80 for an attribute, as listed below.
* *chain*: the chain of the asset or of *party*, see [Chains](#chains).
* *body*: the fields of the entry, as listed below.

The fields of the bodies are:

* *asset* (length-prefixed): an asset on *chain*, encoded as defined in [Encodings](#encodings).
* *bound* (1 byte): 0x00 exact, 0x01 at least, 0x02 at most, 0x03 unbounded.
* *amount* (32 bytes): an unsigned integer in the smallest unit of the asset, in little-endian byte order on every chain; zero if unbounded.
* *party* (length-prefixed): an account on *chain*, encoded as defined in [Encodings](#encodings).
* *name*, *label* (length-prefixed UTF-8 strings): a name or a label of *party*.
* *codeState* (1 byte): the code of *party*: 0x00 no code, 0x01 code, 0x02 a delegation to code, such as by EIP-7702.
* *received* (1 byte): 0x00 if *party* has not received an asset from the payer before, 0x01 if it has.

Integers and length prefixes, other than *chainRefLength*, are minimally encoded CompactSize integers.

| *entryType* | *body* | Entry | Permitted *bound* |
| ----------- | ------ | ----- | ----------------- |
| 0x01 | *asset* \|\| *bound* \|\| *amount* \|\| *party* | *send*: the payer transfers the asset to *party*. | exact, at most, unbounded |
| 0x02 | *asset* \|\| *bound* \|\| *amount* \|\| *party* | *deliver*: the asset is delivered to *party*. | exact, at least |
| 0x03 | *asset* \|\| *bound* \|\| *amount* \|\| *party* | *approve*: *party* may transfer the asset from the payer. | exact, unbounded |
| 0x04 | *asset* \|\| *bound* \|\| *amount* \|\| *party* | *delegate*: *party* obtains authority over the asset, or over every asset of the payer on *chain* if *asset* is empty. | unbounded |
| 0x05 | *asset* \|\| *bound* \|\| *amount* \|\| *party* | *deferred deliver*: a protocol delivers the asset to *party* after the execution of the payload, if it completes the delivery. | exact, at least |
| 0x81 | *party* \|\| *name* | *address name*: *name*, such as an ENS name, resolves to *party*. | |
| 0x82 | *party* \|\| *label* | *label*: *party* is labelled *label* in a registry which the method defines. | |
| 0x83 | *party* \|\| *codeState* | *code state*: the code of *party* is *codeState*. | |
| 0x84 | *party* \|\| *received* | *history*: whether *party* has received an asset from the payer before. | |
| 0x85 | *asset* \|\| *bound* \|\| *amount* \|\| *party* | *existing allowance*: before the payload is executed, *party* may transfer up to *amount* of the asset from the payer; a change of it is an approve. | exact |

### Effects

The effects of a statement account for every security-relevant effect of the payload; a method MUST NOT apply to a payload with a security-relevant effect which the entries above cannot express.
The bound of an effect limits the payer's loss, or for a deliver or a deferred deliver the amount delivered. An approve or a delegate is stated as if it does not lapse.
Fees are outside the statement.

A deferred deliver is made by a protocol after the execution of the payload, such as a bridge which delivers an asset on another chain, or a contract which releases an asset after a delay; every other effect is made by the execution of the payload.
A method states a deferred deliver only under the semantics of the protocol which it covers: its bound is the condition which that protocol enforces, and the deliver holds only if the protocol completes it.
A method MUST NOT apply if the protocol permits an outcome which the effects of the statement cannot express, such as a release of the asset to a party other than the payer.
The trust in such a method includes the trust in the protocol: in its verifier and in the consensus of the chains where the protocol verifies proofs, and in its operators otherwise.

### Attributes

The *key* of an attribute is its *chain* and *name* for an address name; its *chain* and *party* for a code state or a history; and its *chain*, *asset* and *party* for an existing allowance. Its other fields are its *value*. A label has no key, as a *party* MAY have several labels.
A statement MUST NOT contain two attributes of the same type with equal keys and different values.

Which attributes the wallet uses is the policy of the wallet. A wallet which uses an attribute MUST reject the transaction if any other attribute which it is given, established or claimed, is of the same type with an equal key and a different value.

### Validity

The wallet MUST reject a statement which is malformed or non-canonical, has trailing data, violates a constraint of this section, or contradicts facts which the wallet establishes itself, including the metadata it trusts.

## Displaying a statement

A wallet which relies on a statement MUST display:

* its summary, as a description by the party which makes the statement and not as verified;
* every effect, in the order of the encoding, with its bound, an unbounded effect distinguishably from a bounded one and a deferred deliver distinguishably from a deliver;
* the chain of each displayed entry whose *chain* is not the chain of the transaction being signed;
* *amount* and *asset* formatted with metadata which it trusts, or otherwise the asset identifier and the amount in the smallest unit.

The wallet MAY display attributes. It MUST NOT display an attribute in place of the address which it describes.
It MUST display an attribute established by [state proofs](#state-proofs) with the time of the block against whose state it is established, as given by the header of that block, so that the payer can judge its freshness.

## Attestation

An attestation is the concatenation of the following fields:

* *statement* (variable length): the attested statement, encoded as defined in [Statement](#statement).
* *verificationMethod* (4 bytes): 32-bit identifier of the verification method in little-endian byte order, see [Verification methods](#verification-methods).
* *attester* (length-prefixed binary string): identifier of the trust anchor under which the evidence is verified, see [Evidence](#evidence).
* *verificationData* (length-prefixed binary string): method-specific parameters and evidence.

## Payload

The *payload* of an attestation is the list of outputs whose effects its statement describes, in the order in which they appear in the transaction being signed. Where the attestation is used with a payment request, these are the requested outputs of the payment request which are specified by a raw output specification; otherwise they are the outputs which the application designates for the attestation.
The payload need not contain every output of the transaction. The wallet handles every output outside the payload as it would without an attestation, such as by verifying a change output or by displaying the address and the amount of an output.
The wallet computes

```
payloadHash = SHA256(output_1 || output_2 || ... || output_n)
```

from the transaction being signed, where *output*<sub>*i*</sub> is encoded as defined in [SLIP-0024](slip-0024.md), *Signature generation*, for the payload coin type of the chain of the transaction being signed, see [Encodings](#encodings).

The payload commits to the calldata of an EVM output only by its Calldata Digest, see [SLIP-0024](slip-0024.md), *Raw output specifications*; neither the payload nor the attestation contains the calldata.
A method which interprets or simulates the calldata receives it outside the attestation, such as from the application which requests the attestation, and the attester MUST check it against the Calldata Digest before it derives the statement.
As the wallet computes the Calldata Digest from the transaction being signed, a different calldata provided to the attester results in a different *attestationHash*.

### Payload attestations and state assertions

An attestation is either a *payload attestation*, which applies to the payload, or a *state assertion*, which applies to no output, as determined by its *verificationMethod*.
The payload of a state assertion is empty, so its *payloadHash* is SHA256 of the empty string, and its statement MUST have no effects.
The attributes of a state assertion apply to a transaction whose attestation or payment request has the same nonce, as if they were attributes of its statement.

Without a payment request, the wallet MUST verify every attestation which it is given for a method it implements. All payload attestations MUST have the same payload and contain identical effects; the wallet MUST reject the transaction otherwise, and MUST NOT combine the effects of different payload attestations.

## Verification context

The *verification context* encodes the facts outside the payload which can affect the statement. The wallet MUST derive it from the transaction being signed and the payment request, and not from *verificationData*:

```
verificationContext = chain || txType || nonce || methodContext
```

* *chain*: the chain of the transaction, encoded as defined in [Chains](#chains); for an EVM transaction, from its chain ID.
* *txType* (1 byte): the type of the transaction, as defined in [Encodings](#encodings).
* *nonce* (length-prefixed): an unpredictable challenge which the wallet issued.
* *methodContext*: the facts which the verification method defines.

The wallet MUST accept only an attestation whose nonce it issued itself within the current session, and MAY limit how long or for how many transactions it accepts a nonce.

## Attestation hash

```
attestationHash = SHA256(b"\x53\x4c\x28\x41" || verificationMethod || attester || payloadHash || SHA256(verificationContext) || statement)
```

where b"\x53\x4c\x28\x41" is a domain separation tag (this is "SL" followed by 28 in compressed numeric form and "A"), and the fields are encoded as in the attestation.
The wallet computes *attestationHash* itself; it is not part of the attestation.

## Evidence

The evidence in *verificationData* MUST establish every entry of the statement for the payload and the verification context, under a trust anchor which the wallet holds for the method; otherwise the attestation fails verification.

Evidence which commits to *attestationHash*, such as a signature, a proof or an attestation quote over it, establishes the statement by binding it to the payload, the verification context, the method and the attester, provided that it commits to the value which the wallet computed.
Evidence such as [state proofs](#state-proofs) establishes the entries directly: the wallet checks each entry against the evidence itself, and the trust anchor establishes only the state against which it does so.

## Verification methods

A *verification method* determines how the statement is derived and how the attestation is established. Each *verificationMethod* identifies exactly one method, which applies either to payloads or to state assertions.

A method MUST define:

* the payloads or state assertions to which it applies, including the transaction types;
* the evidence in *verificationData* and the trust anchor which *attester* identifies;
* its *methodContext*, which MUST determine every other fact known to the wallet which can affect the statement;
* how it establishes the effects and the attributes of the statement.

### Completeness

A method MUST produce a *complete* statement, whose effects account for every *security-relevant effect* of the payload: any loss of the payer's assets, or any claim on them or on authority over them, now or later.

A statement depends on the payload alone (*interpretation*), on the payload executed against an observed state (*simulation*), or on mutable state (*dynamic state*); where it mixes these, the strongest dependence applies.
An interpretation MUST bound each effect under the semantics which the method covers, and a simulation by a condition which transaction validity or execution enforces; any other effect MUST be stated as unbounded.
A simulation SHOULD include the identifier of the observed state, such as its block hash, in *verificationData*.

### Freshness

Each attribute, and each state which a simulation or a dynamic state observes, MUST be observed after the nonce is issued, as established by evidence bound to the nonce.
Where the wallet verifies the state itself, as with [state proofs](#state-proofs), the state MUST instead be no older than the latest block which the wallet had verified when it issued the nonce.

### Unknown methods

An attestation for a method which the wallet does not implement establishes nothing. The wallet checks its statement, but not its evidence.

### State proofs

With state proofs, the wallet establishes attributes itself, against a state which a trust anchor attests:

```
verificationData = rootEvidence || proofs
```

* *rootEvidence* establishes a block of the chain of the verification context and its state root, under the trust anchor which *attester* identifies, such as a light client checkpoint.
* *proofs* prove values under that state root, in a tree format which the method defines, such as the account and storage proofs of [EIP-1186](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-1186).

A method MUST NOT state an attribute which its proofs do not establish.
On EVM chains, the attributes can be proven as follows:

| Attribute | Proof |
| --------- | ----- |
| code state | The account proof of *party*, with its code where the code hash is not that of empty code. |
| existing allowance | A storage proof in the token contract, at a slot given by a storage layout which the wallet trusts. |
| address name | Storage proofs of the ENS registry and of a resolver which resolves on chain. |
| label | A storage proof in the registry contract. |
| history | A receipt of a transfer from the payer to *party* in an ancestor of the attested block; that *party* has not received from the payer cannot be proven. |

## Use with a payment request

The entries of the statement memo of a [SLIP-0024](slip-0024.md) payment request are claims of the payment request signer, which attestations given with the payment request can establish.
Each such attestation has the nonce of the payment request and is either:

* a payload attestation, whose statement has the summary and all effects of the statement of the memo, and any attributes; or
* a state assertion.

The wallet MUST verify every attestation which it is given for a method it implements, and MUST reject the payment request if such an attestation has another nonce, is neither of the above, or fails verification.

An entry is *established* as follows, and an entry of the memo which is not is *claimed* by the payment request signer:

* the effects are established together by any payload attestation for a method which the wallet implements, which establishes that they are complete;
* an attribute is established by any attestation for a method which the wallet implements and which contains it.

The wallet MUST NOT present the summary or a claimed entry as established.
Which entries must be established before the wallet signs, and by which methods or by how many attestations, is the policy of the wallet.

[SLIP-0024](slip-0024.md) identifies the chain of a payment request by its coin type. The verification context of each attestation and the entries of its statement identify chains exactly, see [Chains](#chains).

## Examples

These examples are informative. They show where the parts of an EVM contract call and the evidence of hypothetical verification methods are placed. The methods, their identifiers and their trust anchors are illustrative and are not registered methods.
The transactions of the examples are [EIP-1559](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-1559) transactions, so *txType* is 0x02.

### Interpretation of a token transfer

The payer transfers 100 USDC to the address *R* on Arbitrum One (chain ID 42161) by calling `transfer(R, 100000000)` on the USDC contract *C*.
The transaction has the destination *C*, the value 0, and 68 bytes of calldata: the selector `a9059cbb`, *R* left-padded to 32 bytes and be32(100000000).

The reading of `transfer(R, x)` as a send of *x* of *C* to *R* is sound only if *C* behaves as a standard token. The payload does not establish that, and *C* is an upgradeable proxy, whose behaviour depends on mutable state.
The hypothetical method therefore covers only contracts in a token list which it trusts, and accepts the current implementation of a proxy in that list as part of that trust; for any other contract it does not apply.
The token list is fixed by the circuit or by the enclave, and is therefore bound by *attester*, which *attestationHash* includes.
Its statement is then an interpretation under the semantics which the method covers, see [Verification methods](#verification-methods).

#### Payload

The calldata is committed to by its [ERC-8213](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-8213) Calldata Digest, which is part of the raw output specification of the single output, encoded as in [SLIP-0024](slip-0024.md), *Signature generation*:

```
calldataDigest = keccak256(be32(68) || calldata)
output_1       = amount || address
amount         = le32(0)
address        = 0x35 || 0x00 || C || calldataDigest
payloadHash    = SHA256(output_1)
```

where 0x35 is the length of *rawOutputType* || *rawOutput* (1 + 20 + 32 bytes) and 0x00 is *rawOutputType*.
The Calldata Digest is therefore committed to by *payloadHash*, and through it by *attestationHash*; the attestation does not contain the calldata. The wallet computes the Calldata Digest, *payloadHash* and *attestationHash* from the transaction being signed.

#### Statement

The statement has a single send effect:

```
statement = 0x11 || "Transfer 100 USDC"             summary
         || 0x01                                    n
         || 0x01                                    entryType: send
         || 0x0000 || 0x02 || 0xa4b1                chain: Arbitrum One
         || 0x14 || C                               asset
         || 0x00                                    bound: exact
         || le32(100000000)                         amount
         || 0x14 || R                               party
```

#### Verification context

Both hypothetical methods interpret the payload alone, so *methodContext* is empty and

```
verificationContext = 0x0000 || 0x02 || 0xa4b1 || 0x02 || 0x20 || nonce
```

for a 32-byte *nonce*.

#### Evidence established by a SNARK

* *attester* is the hash of the verifying key of the circuit. The wallet holds the verifying keys it trusts as its trust anchors for the method and selects the one which *attester* identifies.
* *verificationData* is the proof.
* The public input of the circuit is *attestationHash*, as field elements, as the circuit defines. The private witness is the calldata, the rest of the payload, the verification context and the statement. The circuit computes the Calldata Digest of the calldata in the payload, recomputes *attestationHash* from the witness and *attester*, and proves that the derivation of the method yields the statement from the payload and the calldata.

The wallet verifies the proof under the verifying key with the *attestationHash* which it computed as the public input.
The proof establishes that the derivation was performed correctly, not that *C* behaves as the token list states; that remains a trust in the method.

#### Evidence established by a TEE

* *attester* is the measurement of the code which derives the statement, such as the 32-byte MRENCLAVE of an Intel SGX enclave. The trust anchors of the method are the root key of the TEE vendor and the set of measurements which the wallet accepts.
* *verificationData* is the attestation quote of the enclave, whose report data begins with *attestationHash*.

The wallet verifies the quote up to the root key of the vendor, and checks that the measurement in the quote equals *attester*, that *attester* is a measurement it accepts and that the report data begins with the *attestationHash* which it computed. TCB status and revocation are handled as the method defines.

In both methods, *attester* is included in *attestationHash*, so the evidence cannot be presented under another trust anchor.

### Simulation of a token swap

The payer swaps 1 ETH for USDC on Ethereum (chain ID 1) through the Uniswap V2 router *U*, by calling `swapExactETHForTokens(2450000000, [WETH, T], P, deadline)`, where *T* is the USDC contract and *P* is the payer's address.
The transaction has the destination *U*, the value 10<sup>18</sup> wei, and 228 bytes of calldata.
The statement is derived and attested by a simulation provider which executes the payload against the state of the chain.

#### Payload

```
calldataDigest = keccak256(be32(228) || calldata)
output_1       = amount || address
amount         = le32(10^18)
address        = 0x35 || 0x00 || U || calldataDigest
payloadHash    = SHA256(output_1)
```

#### Statement

The provider executes the payload against a state observed after it receives the nonce. In that state, the swap would deliver, for example, 2503.17 USDC to *P*. That amount is not enforced, as the state can change before the transaction is included, so it MUST NOT be stated as the bound.
The router computes the amount of *T* from the reserves of the pool and reverts if it is less than the `amountOutMin` argument, 2450 USDC; the pool then transfers that amount to *P*. The amount which *P* receives equals it because *T*, by metadata which the provider trusts, takes no fee on transfer and has no transfer hooks. This is the condition which execution enforces, so the bound of the deliver effect is 2450 USDC.
The bound of the send effect is enforced by the validity of the transaction, which transfers exactly its value.
The simulation also establishes that the payload has no other security-relevant effect, such as an approval.

```
statement = 0x13 || "Swap 1 ETH for USDC"           summary
         || 0x02                                    n
         || 0x01                                    entryType: send
         || 0x0000 || 0x01 || 0x01                  chain: Ethereum
         || 0x00                                    asset: native
         || 0x00                                    bound: exact
         || le32(10^18)                             amount
         || 0x14 || U                               party
         || 0x02                                    entryType: deliver
         || 0x0000 || 0x01 || 0x01                  chain: Ethereum
         || 0x14 || T                               asset
         || 0x01                                    bound: at least
         || le32(2450000000)                        amount
         || 0x14 || P                               party
```

The wallet displays:

```
Swap 1 ETH for USDC                (description by the provider)
Send 1 ETH to U
Deliver at least 2450 USDC to P
```

#### Verification context

The execution of the payload depends on its sender, which the wallet knows and the payload does not contain, so the method defines *methodContext* as the 20-byte address of the sender:

```
verificationContext = 0x0000 || 0x01 || 0x01 || 0x02 || 0x20 || nonce || P
```

The observed state is not part of the verification context, as the wallet cannot derive it. The freshness of the observation follows from the nonce, which the provider receives before it observes the state.

#### Evidence established by a provider signature

* *attester* is the public key of the provider, such as a 32-byte Ed25519 public key. The wallet holds the public keys of the providers it trusts as its trust anchors for the method.
* *verificationData* is *blockHash* || *signature*, where *blockHash* is the 32-byte hash of the block whose state the provider observed and *signature* is the signature of the provider over *attestationHash* || *blockHash*.

The wallet verifies the signature under the public key which *attester* identifies, with the *attestationHash* which it computed.
The signature covers *blockHash*, which is not part of *attestationHash*.
Unlike a SNARK or a TEE, the signature establishes only that the provider made the statement; the wallet trusts the provider both to derive the statement correctly and to observe the state after the nonce is issued.

### State proof of a code state

The swap of the previous example is accompanied by a state assertion, with the same nonce, that the router *U* has code. The wallet establishes it itself by [state proofs](#state-proofs), with a light client for Ethereum which had verified the block *B*<sub>0</sub> when the wallet issued the nonce.

#### Statement and payload

```
statement = 0x11 || "Uniswap V2 router"             summary
         || 0x01                                    n
         || 0x83                                    entryType: code state
         || 0x0000 || 0x01 || 0x01                  chain: Ethereum
         || 0x14 || U                               party
         || 0x01                                    codeState: code
```

The payload of a state assertion is empty, so *payloadHash* is SHA256 of the empty string. The method has no *methodContext*:

```
verificationContext = 0x0000 || 0x01 || 0x01 || 0x02 || 0x20 || nonce
```

#### Evidence established by state proofs

* *attester* identifies the checkpoint from which the light client of the wallet was bootstrapped, such as the root of a finalized beacon block. It is only the bootstrap anchor of the light client.
* *rootEvidence* is the chain of sync committee updates from the latest period which the wallet has verified, and the header of a finalized block *B*<sub>1</sub>, together with the proof of the state root of *B*<sub>1</sub> in its header.
* *proofs* is the [EIP-1186](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-1186) account proof of *U* under the state root of *B*<sub>1</sub>, followed by the code of *U*.

The wallet verifies *rootEvidence* with its light client and checks that *B*<sub>1</sub> is later than *B*<sub>0</sub>. This establishes that the state is not older than the light client was when the wallet issued the nonce, not that it was observed after the nonce, see [Verification methods](#verification-methods).
It then verifies the account proof under the state root of *B*<sub>1</sub>, checks the code against the code hash of the account, and checks that the code is not empty and does not begin with 0xef0100, which establishes that *U* has code and is not a delegation.
The evidence establishes the entry of the statement directly; no party signs *attestationHash*. The wallet relies on its light client and on the honesty of the sync committee, and on no attester of the statement.

The attribute applies to the swap, whose attestation has the same nonce. The wallet displays it next to *U*:

```
Swap 1 ETH for USDC                (description by the provider)
Send 1 ETH to U
   U has code (as of block B1, 2026-10-30 12:00 UTC)
Deliver at least 2450 USDC to P
```

### Payment request with established and claimed entries

A merchant requests the payment of the first example by a [SLIP-0024](slip-0024.md) payment request for Arbitrum One, with the recipient name "Shop", *coinType* 9001, the nonce issued by the wallet, and a statement memo:

```
statement = 0x11 || "Transfer 100 USDC"             summary
         || 0x02                                    n
         || 0x01                                    entryType: send
         || 0x0000 || 0x02 || 0xa4b1                chain: Arbitrum One
         || 0x14 || C                               asset
         || 0x00                                    bound: exact
         || le32(100000000)                         amount
         || 0x14 || R                               party
         || 0x81                                    entryType: address name
         || 0x0000 || 0x02 || 0xa4b1                chain: Arbitrum One
         || 0x14 || R                               party
         || 0x08 || "shop.eth"                      name
```

The application gives no payload attestation, so the send effect and the address name are claimed by the merchant, the payment request signer. It gives a state assertion with the nonce of the payment request, signed by a provider as in the second example:

```
statement = 0x08 || "Merchant"                      summary
         || 0x01                                    n
         || 0x83                                    entryType: code state
         || 0x0000 || 0x02 || 0xa4b1                chain: Arbitrum One
         || 0x14 || R                               party
         || 0x00                                    codeState: no code
```

The wallet verifies the state assertion. The code state is established, although the memo does not contain it. The wallet displays:

```
Pay to Shop                        (recipient name)
Transfer 100 USDC                  (description by the merchant)
Send 100 USDC to R                 claimed by the merchant
   shop.eth is R                   claimed by the merchant
   R has no code                   established
```

Whether the wallet signs depends on its policy. A wallet which requires the effects to be established rejects the payment request, and the application can add a payload attestation, such as that of the first example with the nonce of the payment request.

### Interpretation of a bridge transfer

The payer moves 1 ETH from Ethereum to its address *P* on Scroll (chain ID 534352), a zk rollup, by calling `depositETH(P, 10^18, gasLimit)` on the bridge contract *G* of Scroll on Ethereum.
The transaction has the destination *G*, the value 10<sup>18</sup> + *f* wei, where *f* pays for the execution of the deposit on Scroll, and 100 bytes of calldata.

The payment on Ethereum is made by the execution of the payload. The delivery on Scroll is made afterwards by the rollup, which executes the deposit and proves its execution to *G* by a validity proof, so it is a deferred deliver.
The hypothetical method interprets calls to *G* under the semantics of the bridge, which it covers; its bound is the amount in the calldata, which the rollup delivers exactly.
If the rollup does not execute the deposit and the bridge returns the asset to the payer on Ethereum, the payer loses nothing, so the statement need not express that outcome.

#### Payload and statement

```
calldataDigest = keccak256(be32(100) || calldata)
output_1       = amount || address
amount         = le32(10^18 + f)
address        = 0x35 || 0x00 || G || calldataDigest
payloadHash    = SHA256(output_1)
```

```
statement = 0x16 || "Bridge 1 ETH to Scroll"        summary
         || 0x02                                    n
         || 0x01                                    entryType: send
         || 0x0000 || 0x01 || 0x01                  chain: Ethereum
         || 0x00                                    asset: native
         || 0x00                                    bound: exact
         || le32(10^18 + f)                         amount
         || 0x14 || G                               party
         || 0x05                                    entryType: deferred deliver
         || 0x0000 || 0x03 || 0x082750              chain: Scroll
         || 0x00                                    asset: native
         || 0x00                                    bound: exact
         || le32(10^18)                             amount
         || 0x14 || P                               party
```

The verification context is that of Ethereum with an empty *methodContext*, as the recipient is in the calldata:

```
verificationContext = 0x0000 || 0x01 || 0x01 || 0x02 || 0x20 || nonce
```

The evidence is established as in the first example, with a method which covers *G*. For *f* of 0.0001 ETH, the wallet displays:

```
Bridge 1 ETH to Scroll             (description by the attester)
Send 1.0001 ETH to G
Deliver 1 ETH to P on Scroll, later
```

where the payer can tell the deferred deliver from a deliver, and its chain from that of the transaction.

The trust in the method includes the trust in the rollup: in its verifier on Ethereum and in the consensus of Ethereum, and in the liveness of the rollup, which the deliver needs to complete.
With a bridge whose operator attests the transfer, such as Circle's CCTP, the statement has the same form, and the trust in the method includes the trust in that operator.
