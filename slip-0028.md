```

Type:    Standard
Status:  Draft

         Suyash Shandilya <suyash.shandilya@satoshilabs.com>
```

## Abstract

A transaction statement is a human-readable account of the security-relevant effects of the outputs of a transaction, such as the assets which the payer sends or which are delivered to an address. It also contains attributes of the parties and assets involved, such as the name of an address. A wallet displays the statement, or checks it against a policy, before it authorizes the transaction.
An attestation establishes a statement by evidence which the wallet verifies under a trust anchor it holds.
It is issued by a party or mechanism which the wallet trusts for that purpose. This need not be the party that requested the payment.

## Motivation

A wallet can authenticate the destination and the amount of an output, but that does not establish what the output does.
Bitcoin `OP_RETURN` data and EVM calldata are transaction data which an address and an amount do not describe, and whose meaning depends on a protocol the wallet may not implement.
A wallet which cannot establish the effects of such an output can only present it as opaque data (*blind signing*) or refuse to sign it.

This specification defines a statement, which describes those effects in a form the wallet can display or check against a policy.
It also defines an attestation, which lets the statement be verified elsewhere and checked by the wallet.
A statement in a payment request is trusted as far as the payment request signer is. An attestation establishes it independently.

A statement and its attestation are defined independently of any payment request, so that they can also be used when a transaction is signed without one.

## Statement

A statement consists of entries, each of which is an effect or an attribute:

* An *effect* is a change which the successful execution of the payload makes, or causes a protocol to make afterwards, such as a transfer of an asset or an authority granted over it.
* An *attribute* is a fact about a party or an asset which holds when the statement is made, such as the name of an address.

### Format

```
statement = n || entry_1 || ... || entry_n
entry     = entryType || chain || body
```

* *n* (CompactSize integer): the number of entries, which MUST be distinct and in ascending lexicographic order of their encodings.
* *entryType* (1 byte): the type of the entry, below 0x80 for an effect and from 0x80 for an attribute, as listed below.
* *chain*: the chain of the asset or of *party*, see [Chains](#chains).
* *body*: the fields of the entry, as listed below.

The fields of the bodies are:

* *asset* (length-prefixed): an asset on *chain*, encoded as defined in [Encodings](#encodings), or, in a delegate, the single byte 0x00 for every asset of the payer on *chain*.
* *bound* (1 byte): 0x00 exact, 0x01 at least, 0x02 at most, 0x03 unbounded.
* *amount* (32 bytes): an unsigned integer in the smallest unit of the asset, in little-endian byte order on every chain, or zero if unbounded.
* *party* (length-prefixed): an account on *chain*, encoded as defined in [Encodings](#encodings).
* *name*, *label* (length-prefixed UTF-8 strings): a name or a label of *party*.
* *codeState* (1 byte): 0x00 if *party* has no code, 0x01 if it has code, 0x02 if it has a delegation to code, such as by EIP-7702.
* *received* (1 byte): 0x00 if *party* has not received an asset from the payer before, 0x01 if it has.

*n* and the length prefixes are minimally encoded CompactSize integers.

| *entryType* | *body* | Entry | Permitted *bound* |
| ----------- | ------ | ----- | ----------------- |
| 0x01 | *asset* \|\| *bound* \|\| *amount* \|\| *party* | *send*: the payer transfers the asset to *party*. | exact, at most, unbounded |
| 0x02 | *asset* \|\| *bound* \|\| *amount* \|\| *party* | *deliver*: the asset is delivered to *party*. | exact, at least |
| 0x03 | *asset* \|\| *bound* \|\| *amount* \|\| *party* | *approve*: *party* may transfer the asset from the payer. | exact, unbounded |
| 0x04 | *asset* \|\| *bound* \|\| *amount* \|\| *party* | *delegate*: *party* obtains authority over the asset, or over every asset of the payer on *chain* if *asset* is 0x00. | unbounded |
| 0x05 | *asset* \|\| *bound* \|\| *amount* \|\| *party* | *deferred deliver*: a protocol delivers the asset to *party* after the execution of the payload, if it completes the delivery. | exact, at least |
| 0x81 | *party* \|\| *name* | *address name*: *name*, such as an ENS name, resolves to *party*. | |
| 0x82 | *party* \|\| *label* | *label*: *party* is labelled *label* in a registry which the method defines. | |
| 0x83 | *party* \|\| *codeState* | *code state*: the code of *party* is *codeState*. | |
| 0x84 | *party* \|\| *received* | *history*: whether *party* has received an asset from the payer before. | |
| 0x85 | *asset* \|\| *bound* \|\| *amount* \|\| *party* | *existing allowance*: before the payload is executed, *party* may transfer up to *amount* of the asset from the payer. A payload which changes it has an approve effect. | exact |

### Effects

The effects of a statement account for every [security-relevant effect](#completeness) of the payload. A method MUST NOT apply to a payload with a security-relevant effect which the entries above cannot express.
The bound of an effect limits the payer's loss. For a deliver or a deferred deliver, it limits the amount delivered. An approve or a delegate is stated as if it does not lapse.
Transaction fees are outside the statement.

Each effect is worded as follows:

| Effect | Wording |
| ------ | ------- |
| send | Send {*e*} to {*party*} |
| deliver | Deliver {*e*} to {*party*} |
| approve | Allow {*party*} to spend {*e*} |
| delegate | Delegate {*asset*, or the account if *asset* is 0x00} to {*party*} |
| deferred deliver | Deliver {*e*} to {*party*}, later |

{*e*} stands for the bound, the amount and the asset of the effect, such as "at least 2450 USDC", or "any amount of USDC" if it is unbounded.
The amount and the asset are formatted with metadata which the wallet trusts, or otherwise shown as the asset identifier and the amount in the smallest unit.
Where the chain of an effect is not the chain of the transaction being signed, the wallet adds "on {*chain*}" after {*party*}.

A deferred deliver is made by a protocol after the execution of the payload, such as by a bridge which delivers an asset on another chain, or by a contract which releases an asset after a delay. Every other effect is made by the execution of the payload.
A method states a deferred deliver only under the semantics of the protocol which it covers. Its bound is the condition which that protocol enforces, and the deliver holds only if the protocol completes it.
A method MUST NOT apply if the protocol permits an outcome which the effects cannot express, such as a release of the asset to a party other than the payer.
Trust in such a method includes trust in the verifier of the protocol and in the consensus of the chains where it verifies proofs, or otherwise in its operators.

### Attributes

The *key* of an address name is its *chain* and *name*. The key of a code state or a history is its *chain* and *party*. The key of an existing allowance is its *chain*, *asset* and *party*.
The other fields of an attribute are its *value*. A label has no key, as a *party* MAY have several labels.
Two attributes *conflict* if they are of the same type and have equal keys and different values.
Which attributes the wallet uses is the policy of the wallet.

### Correctness

The issuer of a statement, the payment request signer or the issuer of the attestation, is responsible for its correctness, including its canonical encoding and the consistency of its attributes.
The wallet MUST reject a statement which it cannot parse, or whose chains are not canonically encoded, see [Chains](#chains). It MUST reject the transaction if an attribute which it uses conflicts with another attribute which it is given. It MAY reject a statement which contradicts facts which it knows, such as its trusted metadata.

## Displaying a statement

A wallet which relies on a statement MUST display:

* Every effect, worded as in [Effects](#effects), in the order of the encoding. An unbounded effect MUST be distinguishable from a bounded one, and a deferred deliver from a deliver.
* The chain of each displayed attribute whose *chain* is not the chain of the transaction being signed.

The wallet MAY display a title which it derives from the effects, such as "Swap". The wallet MAY display attributes. It MUST NOT display an attribute in place of the address which it describes.
If it displays an attribute established by [state proofs](#state-proofs), it MUST display the time of its block, as given by the block header, so that the payer can judge its freshness.

A wallet without a trusted display, such as an NFC card, MAY instead rely on a statement by a policy which it holds, such as a set of permitted parties and a limit for each asset.
It MUST then rely only on effects which a payload attestation establishes, and MUST NOT sign unless every effect satisfies the policy.

## Attestation

An attestation is the concatenation of the following fields:

* *statement* (variable length): the attested statement, encoded as defined in [Statement](#statement).
* *verificationMethod* (4 bytes): 32-bit identifier of the verification method in little-endian byte order, see [Verification methods](#verification-methods).
* *attester* (length-prefixed binary string): identifier of the trust anchor under which the evidence is verified, see [Evidence](#evidence).
* *verificationData* (length-prefixed binary string): method-specific parameters and evidence.

## Payload

The *payload* of an attestation is the list of outputs whose effects its statement describes, in the order in which they appear in the transaction being signed. With a payment request, these are the requested outputs which are specified by a raw output specification. Otherwise, the application designates them.
The payload need not contain every output of the transaction. The wallet handles every other output as it would without an attestation, such as by verifying a change output or by displaying the address and the amount of an output.
The wallet computes

```
payloadHash = SHA256(output_1 || output_2 || ... || output_n)
```

from the transaction being signed, except with [signing hash binding](#signing-hash-binding). Each *output*<sub>*i*</sub> is encoded as a requested output of [SLIP-0024](slip-0024.md), *Signature generation*, for the payload coin type of the chain, see [Encodings](#encodings).

The payload commits to the calldata of an EVM output only by its Calldata Digest, see [SLIP-0024](slip-0024.md), *Raw output specifications*. Neither the payload nor the attestation contains the calldata.
A method which interprets or simulates the calldata receives it from elsewhere, such as from the application. The issuer of the attestation MUST check it against the Calldata Digest before it derives the statement.
The wallet computes the Calldata Digest from the transaction being signed, so a different calldata given to the issuer results in a different *attestationHash*. With [signing hash binding](#signing-hash-binding), the signing hash commits to the calldata instead.

### Payload attestations and state assertions

An attestation for a method which applies to payloads is a *payload attestation*, which applies to the payload. Its statement has no effects if the payload has no security-relevant effect. An attestation for a method which applies to state assertions is a *state assertion*, which applies to no output, and whose statement has no effects.
The payload of a state assertion is empty, so its *payloadHash* is SHA256 of the empty string.
The attributes of a state assertion apply to the transaction whose payload attestation or payment request has the same nonce, as attributes of its statement.

Without a payment request, the wallet MUST verify every attestation which it is given for a method it implements, and MUST reject the transaction if such an attestation fails verification. All such payload attestations MUST have the same payload and identical effects, and each such state assertion MUST have the nonce of one of them, or the wallet MUST reject the transaction. The wallet MUST NOT combine the effects of different payload attestations.

## Verification context

The *verification context* encodes the facts outside the payload which can affect the statement. The wallet MUST derive it itself, from the transaction being signed and the nonce which it issued, and not from *verificationData*, except with [signing hash binding](#signing-hash-binding):

```
verificationContext = chain || txType || nonce || methodContext
```

* *chain*: the chain of the transaction, see [Chains](#chains). It is given by the chain ID of an EVM transaction, and otherwise by the coin type of the network for which the wallet signs. An EVM transaction without a chain ID has no verification context.
* *txType* (1 byte): the type of the transaction, as defined in [Encodings](#encodings).
* *nonce* (length-prefixed): an unpredictable challenge which the wallet issued.
* *methodContext*: the facts which the verification method defines.

The wallet MUST accept only an attestation whose nonce it issued itself and has not invalidated. It MUST invalidate a nonce when it signs a transaction which relies on it, and MAY invalidate a nonce at any other time, such as upon expiry.

### Signing hash binding

A wallet which receives only the signing hash of a transaction, such as an NFC card, cannot compute the payload or the verification context from the transaction.
A method with *signing hash binding* includes in *methodContext* the hash which the wallet signs, such as keccak256 of the encoding of an EVM transaction. The issuer of the attestation MUST compute that hash from the transaction, and MUST derive the payload, *chain* and *txType* from the same transaction. The method places *payloadHash* in *verificationData*.
Such a method applies only to transactions whose payload contains every output, such as EVM transactions.

With such a method, the wallet MAY take *payloadHash* from *verificationData*, and *chain* and *txType* as given by the application. The signing hash commits to the transaction from which the issuer derived them, so the *attestationHash* which the wallet computes does not match if any of them differs.
The signing hash in *methodContext* MUST be the hash which the wallet signs, and the nonce MUST be the one which it issued. The wallet relies on the issuer of the attestation for the whole transaction, including its destination, value and fees, and needs a new attestation whenever the transaction changes.

## Attestation hash

```
attestationHash = SHA256(b"\x53\x4c\x28\x41" || verificationMethod || attester || payloadHash || SHA256(verificationContext) || statement)
```

where b"\x53\x4c\x28\x41" is a domain separation tag (this is "SL" followed by 28 in compressed numeric form and "A"), and the fields are encoded as in the attestation.
The wallet computes *attestationHash* itself. It is not part of the attestation.

## Evidence

The evidence in *verificationData* MUST establish every entry of the statement for the payload and the verification context, under a trust anchor which the wallet holds for the method. Otherwise, the attestation fails verification.

Evidence which commits to *attestationHash*, such as a signature, a proof or an attestation quote, binds the statement to the payload, the verification context, the method and *attester*. It establishes the statement only if it commits to the value which the wallet computed.
Evidence such as [state proofs](#state-proofs) establishes the entries directly. The wallet checks each entry against the evidence itself, and the trust anchor establishes only the state used for that check.

## Verification methods

A *verification method* determines how the statement is derived and how the attestation is established. Each *verificationMethod* identifies exactly one method, which applies either to payloads or to state assertions.

A method MUST define:

* The payloads or state assertions to which it applies, including the transaction types.
* The evidence in *verificationData* and the trust anchor which *attester* identifies.
* Its *methodContext*, which MUST determine every other fact known to the wallet which can affect the statement.
* How it establishes the effects and the attributes of the statement.

### Completeness

A *security-relevant effect* is any loss of the payer's assets, or any claim on them or on authority over them, now or later.
A method MUST produce a *complete* statement, whose effects account for every security-relevant effect of the payload.

An effect depends on the payload alone (*interpretation*), on the payload executed against an observed state (*simulation*), or on mutable state (*dynamic state*).
An interpretation MUST be bounded under the semantics which the method covers. A simulation MUST be bounded by a condition which transaction validity or execution enforces. Any other effect MUST be stated as unbounded, or as at least 0 for a deliver or a deferred deliver.
For a simulation, the method SHOULD include the identifier of the observed state, such as its block hash, in *verificationData*.

### Freshness

Each attribute, and each state on which an effect depends, MUST be observed after the nonce is issued, as established by evidence bound to the nonce.
Where the wallet verifies the state itself, as with [state proofs](#state-proofs), the state MUST instead be no older than the latest block which the wallet had verified when it issued the nonce.

### Unknown methods

An attestation for a method which the wallet does not implement establishes nothing, and the wallet ignores it.

### State proofs

With state proofs, the wallet establishes attributes itself, against a state which a trust anchor attests:

```
verificationData = rootEvidence || proofs
```

* *rootEvidence* establishes a block of the chain of the attribute and its state root, under the trust anchor which *attester* identifies, such as a light client checkpoint.
* *proofs* prove values under that state root, in a tree format which the method defines, such as the account and storage proofs of [EIP-1186](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-1186).

A method MUST NOT state an attribute which its proofs do not establish.
On EVM chains, the attributes can be proven as follows:

| Attribute | Proof |
| --------- | ----- |
| code state | The account proof of *party*, with its code where the code hash is not that of empty code. |
| existing allowance | A storage proof in the token contract, at a slot given by a storage layout which the wallet trusts. |
| address name | Storage proofs of the ENS registry and of a resolver which resolves on chain. |
| label | A storage proof in the registry contract. |
| history | A receipt of a transfer from the payer to *party* in an ancestor of the attested block. That *party* has not received from the payer cannot be proven. |

## Use with a payment request

The entries of the statement memo of a [SLIP-0024](slip-0024.md) payment request are claims of the payment request signer, which attestations given with the payment request can establish.
Each such attestation has the nonce of the payment request and is either:

* A payload attestation, whose statement has exactly the effects of the statement of the memo, and any attributes.
* A state assertion.

The wallet MUST verify every attestation which it is given for a method it implements. It MUST reject the payment request if such an attestation has another nonce, is neither of the above, or fails verification.
It MUST also reject a payment request which has no statement memo but is given attestations.

An entry is *established* as follows:

* The effects are established together by any payload attestation for a method which the wallet implements, as it establishes that they are complete.
* An attribute is established by any attestation for a method which the wallet implements and which contains it.

An entry of the memo which is not established is *claimed* by the payment request signer.

The wallet MUST NOT present a claimed entry as established.
Which entries must be established before the wallet signs, and by which methods or by how many attestations, is the policy of the wallet.

[SLIP-0024](slip-0024.md) identifies the chain of a payment request by its coin type. Attestations and their statements identify an EVM chain by its chain ID, and any other chain by its coin type, see [Chains](#chains).

## Chains

A chain is identified by its [SLIP-0044](slip-0044.md) coin type, as in [SLIP-0024](slip-0024.md), where that is sufficient, and otherwise by another standard identifier.
A coin type is not a reliable identifier of an EVM chain. An EVM address is the same on every EVM chain, but can hold different code and assets on each.
EVM chains commonly share coin type 60 for key derivation, most of them have no registered coin type, and SLIP-0044 does not map coin types to [EIP-155](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-155) chain IDs.
An EVM chain is therefore identified by its chain ID:

```
chain = chainType || chainRef
```

| *chainType* (1 byte) | Chains | *chainRef* (length-prefixed) |
| -------------------- | ------ | ---------------------------- |
| 0x00 | chains identified by a SLIP-0044 coin type | the 32-bit coin type in little-endian byte order |
| 0x01 | *EVM chains* | the non-zero EIP-155 chain ID as an unsigned integer in big-endian byte order without leading zero bytes, as in the eip155 profile of [CAIP-350](https://github.com/ChainAgnostic/CAIPs/blob/main/CAIPs/caip-350.md) |

An EVM chain MUST be encoded with *chainType* 0x01, and any other chain with 0x00, so that each chain has exactly one encoding. The wallet MUST reject a chain which is encoded otherwise, whose *chainType* is not listed, or which it does not support.
For example, Ethereum is 0x01 || 0x01 || 0x01, Arbitrum One (chain ID 42161) is 0x01 || 0x02 || 0xa4b1, and Bitcoin is 0x00 || 0x04 || 0x00000000.

Coin type 1 is shared by the testnets of all coins, so the testnets of a coin are not distinguished.

### Encodings

This specification defines encodings for EVM chains and for chains of *Bitcoin-like* coin types, whose outputs are specified by a `scriptPubKey`. The wallet MUST reject any other chain.

| Chains | *account* | *asset* | *txType* | Payload coin type |
| ------ | --------- | ------- | -------- | ----------------- |
| EVM | 20-byte address | empty for the native asset, or the 20-byte contract address, followed by the 32-byte big-endian token identifier for a non-fungible or multi-token contract | [EIP-2718](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-2718) type, 0x00 if legacy | an EVM coin type |
| Bitcoin-like | `scriptPubKey` | empty | 0x00 | the coin type of the chain |

Accounts on the same chain are equal if and only if their encodings are. On EVM chains, this includes the contract address in the first 20 bytes of an *asset*.
The payload is encoded as requested outputs of [SLIP-0024](slip-0024.md) for the payload coin type. A payload attestation applies only to a transaction on a chain listed above.

## Examples

These examples are informative. They show where the parts of an EVM contract call and the evidence of hypothetical verification methods are placed. The methods, their identifiers and their trust anchors are illustrative and are not registered methods.
The transactions of the examples are [EIP-1559](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-1559) transactions, so *txType* is 0x02.

### Interpretation of a token transfer

The payer transfers 100 USDC to the address *R* on Arbitrum One (chain ID 42161) by calling `transfer(R, 100000000)` on the USDC contract *C*.
The transaction has the destination *C*, the value 0, and 68 bytes of calldata. The calldata consists of the selector `a9059cbb`, *R* left-padded to 32 bytes and be32(100000000).

Reading `transfer(R, x)` as a send of *x* of *C* to *R* is sound only if *C* behaves as a standard token. The payload does not establish that. Moreover, *C* is an upgradeable proxy, whose behaviour depends on mutable state.
The hypothetical method therefore covers only contracts in a token list which it trusts, including the current implementation of each proxy in that list. It does not apply to any other contract.
The circuit or the enclave fixes the token list, so *attester* binds it, and *attestationHash* includes *attester*.
The send effect is then an interpretation under the semantics which the method covers, see [Completeness](#completeness).

#### Payload

The single output is specified by a raw output specification, which contains the [ERC-8213](https://github.com/ethereum/ERCs/pull/1639) Calldata Digest of the calldata. It is encoded as in [SLIP-0024](slip-0024.md), *Signature generation*:

```
calldataDigest = keccak256(be32(68) || calldata)
output_1       = amount || address
amount         = le32(0)
address        = 0x35 || 0x00 || C || calldataDigest
payloadHash    = SHA256(output_1)
```

where 0x35 is the length of *rawOutputType* || *rawOutput* (1 + 20 + 32 bytes) and 0x00 is *rawOutputType*.
*payloadHash* commits to the Calldata Digest, and *attestationHash* commits to *payloadHash*. The attestation does not contain the calldata. The wallet computes the Calldata Digest, *payloadHash* and *attestationHash* from the transaction being signed.

#### Statement

The statement has a single send effect:

```
statement = 0x01                                    n
         || 0x01                                    entryType: send
         || 0x01 || 0x02 || 0xa4b1                  chain: Arbitrum One
         || 0x14 || C                               asset
         || 0x00                                    bound: exact
         || le32(100000000)                         amount
         || 0x14 || R                               party
```

#### Verification context

Both hypothetical methods interpret the payload under the semantics of their token list, so *methodContext* is empty. For a 32-byte *nonce*:

```
verificationContext = 0x01 || 0x02 || 0xa4b1 || 0x02 || 0x20 || nonce
```

#### Evidence established by a SNARK

* *attester* is the hash of the verifying key of the circuit. The wallet holds the verifying keys it trusts as its trust anchors for the method and selects the one which *attester* identifies.
* *verificationData* is the proof.
* The public input of the circuit is *attestationHash*, as field elements, as the circuit defines. The private witness is the calldata, the payload, the verification context and the statement.
* The circuit checks the calldata against its Calldata Digest in the payload and recomputes *attestationHash* from the witness and *attester*. It proves that the method derives the statement from the payload and the calldata.

The wallet verifies the proof under the verifying key, with the *attestationHash* which it computed as the public input.
The proof establishes that the derivation was correct. It does not establish that *C* behaves as the token list states, which remains a trust in the method.

#### Evidence established by a TEE

* *attester* is the measurement of the code which derives the statement, such as the 32-byte MRENCLAVE of an Intel SGX enclave. The trust anchors of the method are the root key of the TEE vendor and the measurements which the wallet accepts.
* *verificationData* is the attestation quote of the enclave, whose report data begins with *attestationHash*.

The wallet verifies the quote up to the root key of the vendor. It checks that the measurement in the quote equals *attester*, that it accepts *attester*, and that the report data begins with the *attestationHash* which it computed. The method defines how TCB status and revocation are handled.

In both methods, *attestationHash* includes *attester*, so the evidence cannot be presented under another trust anchor.

### Simulation of a token swap

The payer swaps 1 ETH for USDC on Ethereum (chain ID 1) through the Uniswap V2 router *U*, by calling `swapExactETHForTokens(2450000000, [WETH, T], P, deadline)`, where *T* is the USDC contract and *P* is the payer's address.
The transaction has the destination *U*, the value 10<sup>18</sup> wei, and 228 bytes of calldata.
The statement is derived and attested by a simulation provider which executes the payload against the state of the chain.

#### Payload

```
calldataDigest = keccak256(be32(228) || calldata)
output_1       = amount || address
amount         = le32(10^18)
address        = 0x35 || 0x00 || U || calldataDigest
payloadHash    = SHA256(output_1)
```

#### Statement

The provider executes the payload against a state observed after it receives the nonce. In that state, the swap would deliver, for example, 2503.17 USDC to *P*. The state can change before the transaction is included, so that amount is not enforced and MUST NOT be stated as the bound.
The router computes the amount of *T* from the reserves of the pool and reverts if it is less than the `amountOutMin` argument, 2450 USDC. The pool then transfers that amount to *P*. *P* receives exactly that amount, because *T* takes no fee on transfer and has no transfer hooks. The provider knows this from metadata which it trusts. Execution enforces this condition, so the bound of the deliver effect is 2450 USDC.
The validity of the transaction enforces the bound of the send effect, as the transaction transfers exactly its value.
The simulation also establishes that the payload has no other security-relevant effect, such as an approval.

```
statement = 0x02                                    n
         || 0x01                                    entryType: send
         || 0x01 || 0x01 || 0x01                    chain: Ethereum
         || 0x00                                    asset: native
         || 0x00                                    bound: exact
         || le32(10^18)                             amount
         || 0x14 || U                               party
         || 0x02                                    entryType: deliver
         || 0x01 || 0x01 || 0x01                    chain: Ethereum
         || 0x14 || T                               asset
         || 0x01                                    bound: at least
         || le32(2450000000)                        amount
         || 0x14 || P                               party
```

The wallet displays:

```
Send 1 ETH to U
Deliver at least 2450 USDC to P
```

#### Verification context

The execution of the payload depends on its sender, which the wallet knows but the payload does not contain. The method therefore defines *methodContext* as the 20-byte address of the sender:

```
verificationContext = 0x01 || 0x01 || 0x01 || 0x02 || 0x20 || nonce || P
```

The observed state is not part of the verification context, as the wallet cannot derive it. Its freshness follows from the nonce, which the provider receives before it observes the state.

#### Evidence established by a provider signature

* *attester* is the public key of the provider, such as a 32-byte Ed25519 public key. The wallet holds the public keys of the providers it trusts as its trust anchors for the method.
* *verificationData* is *blockHash* || *signature*. *blockHash* is the 32-byte hash of the block whose state the provider observed, and *signature* is the provider's signature over *attestationHash* || *blockHash*.

The wallet verifies the signature under the public key which *attester* identifies, with the *attestationHash* which it computed.
The signature also covers *blockHash*, which is not part of *attestationHash*.
Unlike a SNARK or a TEE, the signature establishes only that the provider made the statement. The wallet trusts the provider both to derive the statement correctly and to observe the state after the nonce is issued.

### State proof of a code state

With the swap of the previous example, the application gives a state assertion that the router *U* has code. It has the same nonce as the swap. The wallet establishes it itself by [state proofs](#state-proofs). Its light client for Ethereum had verified the block *B*<sub>0</sub> when the wallet issued the nonce.

#### Statement and payload

```
statement = 0x01                                    n
         || 0x83                                    entryType: code state
         || 0x01 || 0x01 || 0x01                    chain: Ethereum
         || 0x14 || U                               party
         || 0x01                                    codeState: code
```

The payload of a state assertion is empty, so *payloadHash* is SHA256 of the empty string. The method has no *methodContext*:

```
verificationContext = 0x01 || 0x01 || 0x01 || 0x02 || 0x20 || nonce
```

#### Evidence established by state proofs

* *attester* identifies the checkpoint from which the light client of the wallet was bootstrapped, such as the root of a finalized beacon block. It is only the bootstrap anchor of the light client.
* *rootEvidence* is the chain of sync committee updates from the latest period which the wallet has verified, the header of a finalized block *B*<sub>1</sub>, and the proof of the state root of *B*<sub>1</sub> in its header.
* *proofs* is the [EIP-1186](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-1186) account proof of *U* under the state root of *B*<sub>1</sub>, followed by the code of *U*.

The wallet verifies *rootEvidence* with its light client and checks that *B*<sub>1</sub> is not earlier than *B*<sub>0</sub>. This establishes that the state is not older than the light client was when the wallet issued the nonce. It does not establish that the state was observed after the nonce, see [Freshness](#freshness).
It then verifies the account proof under the state root of *B*<sub>1</sub> and checks the code against the code hash of the account.
Finally, it checks that the code is not empty and does not begin with 0xef0100. This establishes that *U* has code and is not a delegation.
The evidence establishes the entry directly, and no party signs *attestationHash*. The wallet relies on its light client and on the honesty of the sync committee, not on an issuer of the attestation.

The attribute applies to the swap, whose attestation has the same nonce. The wallet displays it next to *U*:

```
Send 1 ETH to U
   U has code (as of block B1, 2026-10-30 12:00 UTC)
Deliver at least 2450 USDC to P
```

### Payment request with established and claimed entries

A merchant requests the payment of the first example by a [SLIP-0024](slip-0024.md) payment request for Arbitrum One, with the recipient name "Shop", *coinType* 9001, the nonce issued by the wallet, and a statement memo:

```
statement = 0x02                                    n
         || 0x01                                    entryType: send
         || 0x01 || 0x02 || 0xa4b1                  chain: Arbitrum One
         || 0x14 || C                               asset
         || 0x00                                    bound: exact
         || le32(100000000)                         amount
         || 0x14 || R                               party
         || 0x81                                    entryType: address name
         || 0x01 || 0x02 || 0xa4b1                  chain: Arbitrum One
         || 0x14 || R                               party
         || 0x08 || "shop.eth"                      name
```

The application gives no payload attestation, so the send effect and the address name are claimed by the merchant, who signed the payment request. The application does give a state assertion with the nonce of the payment request. It is established by a provider signature, as in the second example, under a method for state assertions:

```
statement = 0x01                                    n
         || 0x83                                    entryType: code state
         || 0x01 || 0x02 || 0xa4b1                  chain: Arbitrum One
         || 0x14 || R                               party
         || 0x00                                    codeState: no code
```

The wallet verifies the state assertion. The code state is established, although the memo does not contain it. The wallet displays:

```
Pay to Shop                        (recipient name)
Send 100 USDC to R                 claimed by the merchant
   shop.eth is R                   claimed by the merchant
   R has no code                   established
```

Whether the wallet signs depends on its policy. A wallet which requires established effects rejects this payment request. The application can then add a payload attestation, such as that of the first example with the nonce of the payment request.

### Interpretation of a bridge transfer

The payer moves 1 ETH from Ethereum to its address *P* on Scroll (chain ID 534352), a zk rollup. The payer calls `depositETH(P, 10^18, gasLimit)` on the Scroll bridge contract *G* on Ethereum.
The transaction has the destination *G*, the value 10<sup>18</sup> + *f* wei, and 100 bytes of calldata. *f* pays for the execution of the deposit on Scroll.
The bridge refunds any unused part of *f* to the payer, so the payer sends at most 10<sup>18</sup> + *f*.

The payment on Ethereum is made by the execution of the payload. The delivery on Scroll is made later by the rollup, which executes the deposit and proves it on Ethereum by a validity proof. The delivery is therefore a deferred deliver.
The hypothetical method interprets calls to *G* under the semantics of the bridge. The bound of the deliver is the amount in the calldata, which the rollup delivers exactly.
If the execution on Scroll fails, the deposit can be replayed, and the asset stays locked in the bridge until it succeeds. The asset is never released to another party, so the deliver holds only if it completes.

#### Payload and statement

```
calldataDigest = keccak256(be32(100) || calldata)
output_1       = amount || address
amount         = le32(10^18 + f)
address        = 0x35 || 0x00 || G || calldataDigest
payloadHash    = SHA256(output_1)
```

```
statement = 0x02                                    n
         || 0x01                                    entryType: send
         || 0x01 || 0x01 || 0x01                    chain: Ethereum
         || 0x00                                    asset: native
         || 0x02                                    bound: at most
         || le32(10^18 + f)                         amount
         || 0x14 || G                               party
         || 0x05                                    entryType: deferred deliver
         || 0x01 || 0x03 || 0x082750                chain: Scroll
         || 0x00                                    asset: native
         || 0x00                                    bound: exact
         || le32(10^18)                             amount
         || 0x14 || P                               party
```

The verification context is that of Ethereum with an empty *methodContext*, as the recipient is in the calldata:

```
verificationContext = 0x01 || 0x01 || 0x01 || 0x02 || 0x20 || nonce
```

The evidence is established as in the first example, with a method which covers *G*. For *f* of 0.0001 ETH, the wallet displays:

```
Send at most 1.0001 ETH to G
Deliver 1 ETH to P on Scroll, later
```

The payer can tell the deferred deliver from a deliver, and its chain from that of the transaction.

Trust in the method includes trust in the verifier of the rollup on Ethereum, in the consensus of Ethereum, and in the liveness of the rollup.
The deliver needs that liveness to complete, and the enforced inclusion of transactions from Ethereum supports it.
With a bridge whose operator attests the transfer, such as Circle's CCTP, the statement has the same form, and trust in the method includes trust in that operator.

### Approval and swap in one call

The payer swaps 2500 USDC for ETH on Ethereum through a router *U* in one call.
The call also carries a [Permit2](https://github.com/Uniswap/permit2) permit, signed by the payer, which allows *U* to spend any amount of USDC (*T*).
The approval outlasts the transaction, so it is a security-relevant effect of its own. It is stated as unbounded and as if it does not lapse:

```
statement = 0x03                                    n
         || 0x01                                    entryType: send
         || 0x01 || 0x01 || 0x01                    chain: Ethereum
         || 0x14 || T                               asset: USDC
         || 0x00                                    bound: exact
         || le32(2500000000)                        amount
         || 0x14 || U                               party
         || 0x02                                    entryType: deliver
         || 0x01 || 0x01 || 0x01                    chain: Ethereum
         || 0x00                                    asset: native
         || 0x01                                    bound: at least
         || le32(99 * 10^16)                        amount
         || 0x14 || P                               party
         || 0x03                                    entryType: approve
         || 0x01 || 0x01 || 0x01                    chain: Ethereum
         || 0x14 || T                               asset: USDC
         || 0x03                                    bound: unbounded
         || le32(0)                                 amount
         || 0x14 || U                               party
```

The wallet displays:

```
Send 2500 USDC to U
Deliver at least 0.99 ETH to P
Allow U to spend any amount of USDC
```

### Swap and payment to a merchant

The aggregator swaps the ETH, pays exactly 2400 USDC to *M* and returns the rest, at least 0.01 ETH, to the payer. The call to *A* is the requested output, and the statement memo is:

### Wallet without a display

An NFC card signs the swap of the second example. It has no display, and the phone, which relays its messages, is not trusted. The card receives the signing hash *H* of the transaction, but not the transaction.
The card holds a policy, which permits a send of at most 2 ETH to a party in a list which contains *U*, any deliver to the payer *P*, and no other effect. It also holds the public key of a simulation server as the trust anchor of a method with [signing hash binding](#signing-hash-binding).

The card issues a nonce, which the phone sends to the server with the transaction and its calldata.
The server computes *H* = keccak256(0x02 || RLP of the transaction) and checks the calldata against its Calldata Digest. It derives the statement of the second example, and signs *attestationHash* over the verification context:

```
methodContext       = H || P
verificationContext = 0x01 || 0x01 || 0x01 || 0x02 || 0x20 || nonce || H || P
verificationData    = payloadHash || blockHash || signature
```

The phone gives the card *H*, *chain*, *txType* and the attestation. The card derives the verification context from *H*, its nonce and its own address *P*, takes *payloadHash* from *verificationData*, and computes *attestationHash*.
It verifies the signature under the public key of the server. It then checks the effects against its policy. The send of 1 ETH to *U* and the deliver of at least 2450 USDC to *P* are permitted, so it signs *H* and invalidates the nonce.

If the phone gives the card the signing hash of another transaction, the *attestationHash* which the card computes does not match the one which the server signed, and the card does not sign.
An unbounded approve, or any other effect which the policy does not permit, also makes the card refuse.

The attestation can also come from an enclave. The enclave generates a signing key, and its attestation quote for that key is verified when the card is provisioned. The card then holds the public key of the enclave as the trust anchor, and verifies the attestation as a signature.
The quote itself is not verified on the card, as that needs the collateral of the TEE vendor and a clock.

### Supply and borrow

The payer supplies 10 ETH to a lending pool and borrows 15000 USDC against it in one call. The entries can express a send of 10 ETH, a deliver of the pool's receipt token to the payer, and a deliver of 15000 USDC to the payer.
The loan also gives the pool a claim on the payer's collateral, which it can liquidate. No entry can express this security-relevant effect, so a method MUST NOT apply to this payload, and no attestation establishes its statement.
